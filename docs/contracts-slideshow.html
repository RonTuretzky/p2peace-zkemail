The idea in one slide

Make peace pay, and make aggression cost your own side

① Prove who you are

Citizens prove — in zero knowledge — that they receive DKIM-signed email from their government. No documents, no server, the address never touches chain. One inbox ↔ one member

② Stake into peace

Every citizen mint puts 10% into their community's pool — an opt-in stake explicitly at risk against their own side's aggression. Never anyone's untouched savings

③ Let the world attest

Any newsletter subscriber on earth can prove a news event happened from a saved .eml. Confirmed events move pool value after a dispute window. No notary to bribe or censor

Two communities (A and B), two reserve-backed tokens, one shared Treasury. The contracts encode only what both sides separately agree to — neither can impose rules on the other.

Foundational choice

Why zkEmail instead of zkTLS

zkTLS (the original sketch)

  • A notary/MPC network must attest the TLS session live, at fetch time.
  • Prover + notary both online during the session; transcript is unrepeatable.
  • The notary is a censorable, bribable choke point.
  • Conflict-zone governments will never expose identity APIs to attest against.

zkEmail (what we built)

  • Trust anchor is the DKIM signature the sender's mail server already produced.
  • Nobody need be online — any recipient proves after the fact from a saved .eml.
  • Evidence is archived in millions of inboxes; proofs can be made years later.
  • Governments already send DKIM-signed mail (tax receipts, ID-portal notices).

On-chain, a proof is verified as a Groth16 SNARK against a registry of DKIM public keys. The email body and address never appear on-chain — only a nullifier.

Proof layer · the shared primitive

One proof envelope, two jobs

struct EmailProof { bytes32 dkimPubkeyHash; // which DKIM key signed bytes32 domainHash; // keccak(sender domain) bytes32 nullifier; // anti-replay handle bytes32 patternHash; // pins the exact circuit uint64 emailTimestamp; // DKIM-covered Date uint256[8] proof; // Groth16 πA·πB·πC }

ZKEmailVerifier.verify() routes by patternHash to the Groth16 verifier compiled for that blueprint, then checks the key against DKIMRegistry.

Citizenship proof

Claim: "I control an inbox that gets DKIM-signed mail from government domain D."

nullifier = Poseidon(recipientAddress, salt) — constant per inbox ⇒ one registration per person.

News-event proof

Claim: "A DKIM-signed email from news domain D, at time T, matches keyword pattern P."

nullifier = Poseidon(dkimSignature) — unique per physical email ⇒ each newsletter counts once.

Proof layer · trust anchor

DKIMRegistry & ZKEmailVerifier

DKIMRegistry — the key archive

DNS only serves a domain's current key. An on-chain archive with validity windows is what lets old emails stay provable and leaked keys get killed.

setKey() · revokeKey() · isKeyValid()

Key additions go through timelocked governance. Revocation is deliberately fast — owner or guardian, no timelock: a leaked mail-server key is a forgery weapon.

ZKEmailVerifier — the router

Maps each patternHash → the Groth16 verifier compiled for that blueprint. Verifies key validity, then the SNARK over 6 public signals.

setVerifier() · verify(proof, extraData)

The 6th signal (extraData) is the caller's context: the target wallet for identity, 0 for events — this binds a proof to its use so it can't be redirected.

New circuits drop in as new verifiers; nothing else in the system changes when blueprints are added or upgraded.

Proof layer · the member roll

IdentityRegistry — sybil-resistant citizenship

register(EmailProof, wallet) — anyone may relay a proof; the enrolled wallet is baked inside the proof, so a stolen proof can't be redirected.

  • Domain must be allowlisted for a community (governance).
  • Pattern must be an approved citizenship blueprint.
  • Email must be fresh (≤ 90d) and not from the future.
  • Strictly-increasing timestamp per nullifier ⇒ every proof is single-shot.

Three modes, one function

Enrollfresh nullifier → new member, pool slot created Renewsame wallet + fresh email → extend 365d membership Rotatesame inbox, new wallet → lost-key recovery; unclaimed rewards follow the nullifier

Sybil model, stated honestly: one identity per government email account — not strictly one per human, but most portals bind one address per national ID.

Economy layer · issuance

PeaceToken & PeaceMinter

Two ERC-20s (one per community), fully reserve-backed: the minter holds one unit of reserve (demo: MockUSD) per token. reserve == totalSupply always.

Citizen mint · 1:1

mintCitizen(usd) — 90% of tokens to you, 10% staked into your CommunityPool.

Buying in is signing the rebalancing agreement.

Outsider mint · 2× premium

mintOutsider(usd) — par half backs tokens, the premium half funds the Treasury.

Same redemption rights; no pool stake, no vote.

Redeem · 1:1

redeem(tokens) — burn for reserve, no questions, while reserve lasts.

mintAtPar() is the protocol-internal path used by the engine & escrow.

Only its own PeaceMinter can mint or burn a token — every unit in existence is redeemable, including tokens that redistribution moves between pools.

Economy layer · the load-bearing redesign

CommunityPool — two strictly separate buckets

Corpus — the stake at risk

The 10% from every citizen mint. Never claimable. Capital consented to be slashed if this community commits a harmful act.

only RedistributionEngine can move it → slashCorpus()

Rewards — the peace dividend

Inflows from finalized events, treasury payouts, escrow releases. Claimable by verified members, an equal share each.

accumulator pattern · claim() keyed by nullifier

Rewards use a classic accumulator (accRewardPerMember grows on inflow; members claim their delta). Equal-per-member — not pro-rata by wealth — is deliberately democratic, and only possible because identities are sybil-resistant.

Why not confiscate arbitrary holder balances? You can't iterate holders fairly, and seizing individuals' savings for state actions they may oppose is collective punishment — the very thing the protocol exists to end. So the stake is opt-in.

The proposal pins a circuit, not prose

A proposal commits a patternHash (the exact compiled zk-regex), a set of approved source domains tagged A / B / Intl, distinct-source thresholds, an attestation window, and a capped redistributionBps (≤5%). Voters approve a circuit.

Why quadratic finally works here

n votes lock n² tokens of your community's PeaceToken (refunded after). Token-weighted quadratic voting is sybil-vulnerable without identity — split tokens across wallets and the penalty vanishes. zkEmail identity is what makes it real: ballots are per-nullifier.

Dual majority to pass: YES>NO within A and within B, and ≥30% of the roll participated. Neither side can impose rules on the other.

Execution layer · the oracle

EventAttestation — permissionless, multi-source

Anyone holding a DKIM-signed email from an approved source, matching the incentive's committed pattern, calls attest(incentiveId, proof). Proofs cluster into rounds = one candidate event.

Rounds

The first proof opens a round; later proofs must fall within the attestation window of the first ("reports within 7 days = the same event").

Distinct-source tally

One count per outlet per round, one count per physical email. A round confirms when it has ≥ required A, B, and Intl sources.

On confirm → hand off

Bumps the incentive's trigger bookkeeping and calls engine.onEventConfirmed(), opening the dispute window.

Anti-abuse: 30-day submission lag, per-round dedup, per-incentive cooldown + maxTriggers so a recurring event can't drain a pool. Guardian can pause attestation in emergencies.

Harmful by X

Slash pool X corpus → redeem 1:1 to USD at minter X → mint 1:1 into pool Y rewards. Reserve backing preserved on both sides.

Positive by X

Reward paid from the shared Treasury into pool X — rewarding de-escalation must not itself punish the other side.

Joint

Treasury USD split 50 / 50 into both pools.

finalize() is permissionless once the window passes; amounts are recomputed against the live corpus (a prior slash can shrink it — hence the min()).

Execution layer · the rest

Circuit breaker, external funds, commerce

DisputeCouncil

A balanced, multi-stakeholder council. voteReverse(eventId) — and nothing else. Reversal needs YES from ≥75% of the entire council, and only during the open window.

A reversed event returns its trigger slot but keeps the cooldown clock.

SanctionsEscrow

States / NGOs / diaspora deposit() reserve against a specific incentive + beneficiary + expiry. On a finalized event the tranche release()s; else the donor reclaim()s after expiry.

"Tokenized sanctions relief" — transparent, immutable conditions.

BusinessRegistry

Merchants certified by a simple dual-majority poll (revocable the same way). Paying a certified business across the conflict line earns a Treasury cooperation bonus.

Bonus is budgeted per epoch (~1%/mo) to contain wash-trading.

Putting it together

One harmful event, end to end

1. Citizen of A proves gov email → IdentityRegistry.register() → enrolled 2. Mints tokens → PeaceMinter.mintCitizen() → 10% into CommunityPool A corpus 3. Anyone proposes an incentive → IncentiveRegistry.propose() → "if [pattern] reported, slash A 5%" 4. Members of A & B vote quadratically → castVote() → finalize() → dual majority passes → Active 5. Subscribers prove newsletters → EventAttestation.attest() × sources → round Confirmed 6. Engine snapshots the amount → onEventConfirmed() → 48h dispute window opens 7a. Council disagrees → DisputeCouncil.voteReverse() ≥75% → Reversed, nothing moves 7b. Window passes → RedistributionEngine.finalize() → corpus A → rewards B 8. Members of B claim their equal share → CommunityPool.claim() → peace dividend paid 8+. Any escrowed tranche on this incentive → SanctionsEscrow.release() → relief funds flow

Value only ever moves at step 7b — after proof, after consent, after a standing dispute window. Everything before it is reversible.
