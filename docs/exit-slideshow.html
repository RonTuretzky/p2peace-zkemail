The Shekel Exit

How two communities move economic life out of a national currency they can't control — provably, privately, and without anyone getting hurt for taking part.

A walk through what we built: the honest mechanism, the proof, the privacy, and the sovereignty layer — and a clear line between what's real today and what still needs a circuit.

the question you asked

"Can we make a mechanism to reduce the value of the currency?"

The intuitive answer — sell the shekel to push its price down — turns out not to be real. Two hard reasons, not fixable by any protocol:

  • You cannot sell a fully-backed stablecoin below its peg — arbitrageurs redeem it at par for free profit until it restores.
  • You cannot move a free-floating currency's FX rate — the shekel trades tens of billions/day, backed by ~$232B in reserves, and the central bank is currently fighting the opposite problem.

A contract that "dumps shekels to weaken them" would be theater. So we built the mechanism that is real.

the real mechanism

Demand destruction, not price manipulation

A currency's value rests on demand to hold and use it. Every unit of economic life that moves from shekels into community-money-on-stablecoin is:

  • demand for the shekel that no longer exists
  • a balance the central bank can't inflate or tax — seigniorage denied
  • monetary sovereignty transferred from the state to the two communities
The Exit Index. It's slow, real, cumulative — and measurable on-chain without holding or selling a single shekel. That's the honest version of "devaluing the currency."
proving it — the good news

A conversion is a signed fact

When you convert shekels to crypto on an exchange, it emails you a receipt — and that email is cryptographically signed by the exchange's own mail servers (DKIM). We verify that signature fully on-chain.

Your real Bit2C withdrawal email proved it: the DKIM signature, the body, and the full destination address (0xc3aD…946C) all verify on-chain. Its own words: "to myself and an address I own."

  • Reuses the same RSA/DKIM machinery as the identity path — no new crypto
  • The address is bound — the receipt can only ever count for the wallet it names (not a bearer instrument)
the honest ceiling

Proves a conversion — not a net exit

A signed receipt survives three attacks that no proof anywhere can close, because they're economic, not cryptographic:

  • Round-trip: convert, save the email, buy shekels back an hour later — the receipt is valid forever
  • Borrowed shekels: convert an overdraft — a flawless receipt, zero net exit
  • Provenance: the email can't tell savings from money deposited that morning to mint a receipt
So we measure the stock, not the flow. The load-bearing number is the sDAI held right now (round-trip-resistant by construction); the receipt rides alongside as labeled provenance, never the accounting.
privacy — your first ask

Prove it without revealing the address

On-chain DKIM verification is authentic but public — the signed email (with your address) sits in calldata forever. Hiding the address isn't a tweak; it means moving verification inside a zero-knowledge proof.

Public path real crypto

Verifies the real Bit2C signature + body + address entirely on-chain. Genuine — but reveals the address, and a 37 KB email exceeds the block gas limit as a tx.

Private path (ZK) mock today

Reveals only a nullifier. Address, amount, email never touch the chain. Bound to your wallet; the nullifier folds a wallet secret so even the exchange can't recompute it.

sovereignty — your deeper ask

The last link: the KYC wallet

Bit2C settles the withdrawal to W_kyc — an address KYC-bound to a regulated exchange, plausibly visible to the state. Any move from it into a p2p2p contract puts the citizen on the peace-protocol graph. That can be dangerous.

ZK-over-email hides the email, not the wallet that transacts. So we go to Sepolia / Ethereum — where Bit2C actually settles — to drop the bridge, and add a shielded pool.

The elegant part: entry is gated by the Bit2C proof, so the anonymity set is "provably-real shekel exits" — Vitalik's Privacy-Pools association-set idea, giving sovereignty and honest provenance at once.

The honest line: demo vs. real

real today

  • The pool structure: Poseidon Merkle tree, nullifier double-spend, single-use vouchers, fixed denomination, untrusted relayer
  • The withdraw ZK circuit is REAL — a compiled Groth16 Merkle-membership proof, verified on-chain, live on Sepolia. The mock is gone.
  • The anonymous, member-less sink — no wallet ever on a p2p2p contract

still demo-tier

  • The trusted setup is single-party — production needs a multi-party ceremony (no one holds the toxic waste)
  • The provenance gate is still a mock — proving "real Bit2C exit" in ZK needs the heavy zkEmail circuit (RSA + SHA-256 + regex)
  • Real safety also needs fixed denoms, a min anonymity-set size, a delay, and a permissionless relayer
Bottom line: the load-bearing anonymity circuit is done — real proofs verify on-chain. What's left is a proper ceremony, the zkEmail provenance circuit, and a real relayer — not the core cryptography.

what's next

What's left (the honest remainder)

  • Withdraw circuit — DONE. Compiled, deployed, real proofs verify on-chain (Sepolia).
  • The zkEmail provenance circuit — RSA + SHA-256 + regex over the email; the one heavy circuit left, so "these are all real Bit2C exits" is proven, not asserted
  • A multi-party ceremony to replace the single-party trusted setup
  • A real relayer (permissionless / 4337 over Tor) + client-side proof gen for the shielded UI, and continuous volume to grow the anonymity set
The throughline: at every step we built the real thing where we could, and labeled the gap honestly where we couldn't. No theater — a measurable mechanism, an authentic email proof, a real privacy model, and a sovereignty layer whose load-bearing ZK circuit now verifies real proofs on-chain.
